Reread or Test Yourself? Reading a Memory Chart

Three classmates in a study group talk about a chart from their psychology class experiment before writing a paragraph about it.

Name: ______________________   Date: ____________   Score: ____ / 12

1. Before you listen: key words

strategy nouna plan or method for doing something
process nounthe steps or actions you take to do something
retain verbto keep or continue to have
benefit noun / verba good or helpful effect
decline verb / nounto become lower or worse
significantly adverbby a large or important amount (in research: not by chance)
plateau verb / nounto stop rising and stay at the same level
outcome nouna result
variable adjectivechanging; different in different cases
consecutive adjectivefollowing one after another without a break

2. While you listen: notes

Listen for signposts (first, but, the main reason is, so to sum up). Write key words, not sentences.

Trend 1 (numbers)
Trend 2 (numbers)
Explanations they give
Do they agree? Final idea

3. Questions

1. What is the conversation mainly about?

  1. The three lines on the chart, and how the study sessions were timed
  2. The three lines on the chart, and how far they can trust the result
  3. The three lines on the chart, and how Sofia should study for her midterm
  4. The three lines on the chart, and which line surprised them the most

2. What does Josh say about the spaced group's three sessions?

  1. Each session was twenty minutes long
  2. The sessions were on Monday, Wednesday and Friday
  3. Each session was shorter than the one before
  4. The last session was the longest one

3. What does Sofia's question about the professor's Friday quiz show about her?

  1. She thinks the Friday flashcard quiz is a waste of class time
  2. She has read the handout more carefully than the others
  3. She wants the group to study with flashcards before the midterm
  4. She is guessing from what happens in class, not from the chart

4. How does Mei first respond to Sofia's idea?

  1. She says Sofia is wrong and points to day one
  2. She partly agrees, then points to day thirty
  3. She agrees and asks Josh to go on
  4. She asks Josh to read all the numbers first

5. Josh corrects one number while he reads the rereading line. Which one?

  1. Day two: he says sixty-five, then changes it to sixty
  2. Day four: he says forty, then changes it to forty-five
  3. Day seven: he says thirty, then changes it to thirty-five
  4. Day fourteen: he says thirty-five, then changes it to thirty

6. Sofia turns a percent on the chart into a number of words. Which is correct?

  1. Rereading on day one: she says thirty, then thirty-two
  2. Self-testing on day thirty: she says eighteen, then twenty
  3. Spaced self-testing on day thirty: she says twenty, then twenty-four
  4. Rereading on day thirty: she says ten, then twelve

7. What is Mei's main point about the chart?

  1. Rereading works for a test tomorrow, but not next month
  2. Self-testing works for a test tomorrow, but not next month
  3. Spaced testing starts lowest, but it's best after a month
  4. When you test yourself matters, not only whether you do

8. What is Josh's main worry about the experiment?

  1. There were only ten people in each group
  2. Some students may already have known some of the words
  3. Nobody checked how long each student really studied
  4. The students chose which group to be in

9. Why does Josh talk about students who are great at languages?

  1. To show that one strong student can change a small group's score
  2. To show that one group may have had stronger students from the start
  3. To show that strong students may already have known some of the words
  4. To show that strong students may not have studied for the full hour

10. How does Mei respond after Sofia agrees with Josh?

  1. She agrees with both of them and wants to change the paragraph
  2. She says the gap on day thirty is too big to be luck
  3. She points out that the spaced group was lowest on day one
  4. She says bigger studies have found the same pattern

11. What will Sofia probably do for the midterm?

  1. Reread her notes on three days in a row
  2. Test herself with flashcards the night before
  3. Reread her notes the night before, like last semester
  4. Test herself with flashcards on three days in a row

12. How do Mei and Josh react to Sofia's idea at the end?

  1. Mei starts to object, but Josh defends Sofia
  2. Josh starts to object, but Mei defends Sofia
  3. Mei starts to object, and Josh adds a second problem
  4. Josh starts to object, and Mei adds a second problem

4. After you listen: discuss

  1. How do you usually study new vocabulary? After listening, would you change anything?
  2. Josh worries that students chose their own groups. Why could that make an experiment less fair?
  3. Have you ever felt sure you knew something, then forgot it on a test? What happened?

Transcript

Mei: Alright, our paragraph about this chart from psychology class is due Thursday. Josh, can you start? You actually read the handout.

Josh: Ha, yeah, I did. So, it's our class experiment. The participants learned forty new vocabulary words. Then they were tested six times, up to thirty days later.

Sofia: And the three lines are three different study methods, right?

Josh: Right. First, rereading. You just read the word list again and again. Second, self-testing. Like flashcards: you cover the meaning and try to remember it. And third, spaced self-testing. Same thing, but in three short sessions on three consecutive days.

Mei: Consecutive... so, like, Monday, Wednesday, Friday?

Josh: No, one day after another. Monday, Tuesday, Wednesday. And everyone studied for the same total time. One hour.

Sofia: So twenty minutes a day for the spaced group?

Josh: That's what I thought too. But no. Thirty minutes on the first day, then twenty, then only ten. The idea is, you need less time as you remember more.

Mei: Huh. Shorter every day. Interesting.

Sofia: Okay. Honestly, I can already tell you the result. Self-testing wins. Why else would the professor give us a flashcard quiz every Friday?

Mei: Actually, no. You're wrong. Look at day one first. Rereading is the highest. Eighty percent.

Sofia: Eighty?

Mei: Yeah, eighty. Compared to seventy-five for self-testing, and only sixty-five for the spaced group.

Sofia: Oh. So on day one, the spaced group is actually the lowest.

Mei: Exactly. That's the surprising part. If the study stopped on day one, we'd all say, "Rereading is the best strategy."

Sofia: Okay, but that's just day one.

Josh: Mm-hm. Then it changes fast.

Mei: So what happens to rereading after day one?

Josh: It drops sharply. It's sixty on day two, forty-five on day four, thirty... sorry, thirty-five on day seven... by day fourteen, it's down to thirty.

Sofia: And after that?

Josh: After that, it levels off. It just plateaus. On day thirty, it's still thirty percent.

Sofia: Thirty percent of forty words... that's, um, ten words. No, wait. Twelve words.

Mei: Twelve out of forty. That's not a lot.

Sofia: Okay, but that still proves my point. Self-testing is better.

Josh: Well, yes and no. Self-testing goes down too, but more slowly. It's a steady decline. Seventy-five, seventy, sixty-five... and fifty by day thirty.

Mei: And look at the spaced group. That's my favorite line.

Josh: Yeah, that one's almost flat. It stays at sixty-five for the first week. Then it goes down just a little, to sixty.

Sofia: Wait. So on day thirty, spaced self-testing is the highest?

Josh: Yep. Sixty percent, compared to fifty for normal self-testing and thirty for rereading.

Sofia: Oh. I thought the two self-testing lines were basically the same.

Mei: Not really. See, the two lines meet at day four. They're both at sixty-five. After that, the spaced line overtakes the other one.

Sofia: Hmm. So it's not only about testing yourself. When you test yourself matters too.

Mei: Right. But the main point is, rereading isn't useless. It's great if your test is tomorrow. It's just bad for a test next month.

Josh: Okay. Can I be the annoying one for a second?

Sofia: Go ahead.

Josh: So, first, the sample is small. Only thirty participants, so ten people in each group. But my real worry is something else. The students chose their own group.

Mei: Wait, they chose? The professor didn't put them in groups?

Josh: Nope. And the professor said the scores were really variable from person to person. For example, maybe the students who are great at languages all picked flashcards.

Sofia: That's fair. Maybe the spaced group just had stronger students.

Mei: Maybe, but I don't think so. Think about day one. If they were stronger students, they'd be the highest on day one. But they were the lowest. Sixty-five.

Josh: Hmm. Good point. And the professor said the spaced group did significantly better by day thirty. Bigger studies found the same pattern too. I just think we should mention how the groups were chosen.

Sofia: Okay, so to sum up. Rereading feels good at first, but it declines fast, so you don't retain much. Self-testing is better. And spaced self-testing gives the best outcome after a month.

Mei: Plus Josh's point. The students picked their own groups.

Josh: Thank you.

Sofia: Okay, it's not as obvious as I said. Last semester I just read my textbook again and again the night before every test.

Mei: Me too. But that's the benefit of spacing. You don't need more time. You just change the process.

Sofia: Hmm. Our midterm's in three weeks, right? So if I read my notes on Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday... that's consecutive, right?

Mei: Well, yes, it's consecutive. But reading isn't testing yourself. You need to...

Josh: Hey, come on. It's better than one night.

Mei: Ha. Okay, okay. That's true. It's a start.

Sofia: Thank you, Josh.
