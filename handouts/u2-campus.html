EIL313 Listening Practice · Unit 2 · Campus conversation

Five Out of Fifteen

In the campus library, Josh has just got a psychology quiz back. His friend Mei and a librarian talk with him about it.

Name: ______________________   Date: ____________   Score: ____ / 12

1. Before you listen: key words

strategy nouna plan or method for doing something
process nounthe steps or actions you take to do something
retain verbto keep or continue to have
benefit noun / verba good or helpful effect
motivated adjectivewanting to do something and working hard to do it
persistence nouncontinuing to try even when something is difficult
misconception nouna wrong idea or belief
technique nouna special way of doing something
task nouna piece of work you have to do
deliberate adjectivedone on purpose, with a clear plan

2. While you listen: notes

Listen for signposts (first, but, the main reason is, so to sum up). Write key words, not sentences.

Problem / situation
Opinions (who thinks what?)
Reasons & examples
Decision / next step

3. Questions

1. What is Ms. Okafor's main message to Josh?

  1. Pulling ideas from memory matters more than rereading them
  2. Spreading study over days matters more than one long night
  3. Getting enough sleep matters more than studying late
  4. How he uses study time matters more than how much

2. What score did Josh expect to get on the quiz?

  1. Twelve out of fifteen
  2. Thirteen out of fifteen
  3. Fourteen out of fifteen
  4. Fifteen out of fifteen

3. How does Josh react when Mei jokes about his yellow highlighter?

  1. He asks her not to laugh at him
  2. He laughs too and says, "Fine."
  3. He says that he didn't use a highlighter
  4. He jokes back that her method is slow

4. What does Ms. Okafor do first, after Josh tells her what happened?

  1. She asks him to explain chapter four to Mei
  2. She asks him how the rereading felt
  3. She asks him what time he went to bed
  4. She asks about chapter four without his notes

5. Why does Ms. Okafor say "See?" after Josh's answer about chapter four?

  1. She expected his answer and is using it to make her point
  2. She is pleased that he remembers so many small details
  3. She wants him to admit that he never read chapter four
  4. She wants Mei to explain chapter four to him instead

6. Ms. Okafor uses the word "misconception." Which idea does she call a misconception?

  1. Thinking that he has a bad memory
  2. Thinking that more hours always mean more learning
  3. Thinking that good studying should feel easy
  4. Thinking that the quiz questions were unfair

7. When Josh says Mei's method sounds slow, what is her main point?

  1. It's hard, but it only takes the ten minutes before each class
  2. It's hard, but it only takes one page for each lecture
  3. It's hard, but it only takes the twenty minutes of her bus ride
  4. It's hard, but it only takes one hour on Sunday nights

8. Ms. Okafor says, "The main reason it works is this." What is the main reason?

  1. Pulling information out of memory makes that memory stronger
  2. The blank page shows what you don't know, so you retain more
  3. Planned gaps between sessions make memories last longer
  4. Sleep after studying makes the day's memories stronger

9. What does Mei mean when she says, "You found four hours last night"?

  1. Four hours in one night is far too long to study
  2. He should give up choir practice to make more time
  3. He should study with her on the bus after class
  4. He does have time to study, so that excuse is weak

10. Why does Josh say he feels a little better?

  1. Mei tells him that he isn't hopeless
  2. The next quiz is still two weeks away
  3. Ms. Okafor says the quiz doesn't count much
  4. Ms. Okafor once got a low score too

11. In his plan, how often will Josh quiz himself on the old chapters?

  1. Once a week
  2. Three times a week
  3. Twice a week
  4. After every class

12. How does Mei answer when Josh asks her to quiz him?

  1. She says yes, if he checks her history essay in return
  2. She says yes, if he buys them both coffee in return
  3. She says yes, if he joins her Wednesday study group in return
  4. She says yes, if he quizzes her for her history quiz in return

4. After you listen: discuss

  1. How do you usually study for a quiz? After listening, would you change anything? Why?
  2. Josh felt "I know this" when he reread his notes. Have you ever felt ready for a test and then found it hard? What happened?
  3. In your culture or family, what advice do people give about studying and sleep? Does it match what Ms. Okafor said?

Transcript

Mei: Josh? Are you okay? You've been staring at that paper for ages.

Josh: Not really. It's my psychology quiz. I got five out of fifteen.

Mei: Five? Oh, Josh. I'm sorry.

Josh: And I studied! I reread all my notes last night. For four hours. Until two in the morning. I was sure I'd get fifteen. Well... fourteen.

Mei: Fourteen? Wow. Four hours of rereading. Let me guess... with your yellow highlighter?

Josh: Okay, don't laugh. It's not funny.

Mei: Sorry, sorry. Hey, Ms. Okafor's at the help desk. She runs the study-skills workshops. Ms. Okafor? Do you have a minute?

Ms. Okafor: Sure, Mei. What's going on?

Josh: Hi, I'm Josh. I did badly on a quiz. I think I need more time. Next time I'll study six hours instead of four.

Ms. Okafor: Maybe. But first, a quick question. While you were rereading, how did it feel?

Josh: Good. Everything looked familiar. I kept thinking, "Yeah, I know this."

Ms. Okafor: Okay. Now close your notebook. What was the main idea of chapter four?

Josh: Uh... I can see the page. There's a blue heading at the top. And a picture of a brain on the left. But the words... No. I don't know.

Ms. Okafor: See? You remember what the page looked like, but not what it said.

Josh: Maybe I just have a bad memory.

Ms. Okafor: No. Your memory is fine. Thinking you have a bad memory is a very common misconception. A misconception is a wrong idea. The real problem is your method.

Josh: My method? What do you mean?

Ms. Okafor: When you reread, it feels familiar. Because of that, you feel confident. But familiar isn't the same as remembering. The quiz gave you a different task: pull the answer out of your own head.

Josh: Huh. Okay. That's kind of scary.

Mei: Now you know why I quiz myself.

Ms. Okafor: Tell him what you do, Mei.

Mei: Well, first, on the bus home after class, I close my notes. I write everything I remember on a blank page. Then I check my notes for what I missed.

Josh: That sounds slow.

Mei: It's not slow. It's hard! But the bus ride is only twenty minutes. So that's all it takes.

Ms. Okafor: And the hard part is the point. The main reason it works is this: the blank page shows you what you don't know yet. So you spend your time on the gaps, and you retain more. Also, each time you pull information out of memory, it gets stronger. We call this technique retrieval practice.

Josh: So the struggling is... good?

Ms. Okafor: Exactly. A little struggle is good for you.

Mei: And the second thing I do: I don't do it all on one day. I go back to the same chapter a few days later. And then again the next week.

Ms. Okafor: That's called spacing. It's a deliberate strategy. You plan the gaps. You forget a little, so remembering takes more effort. As a result, the memory lasts longer.

Josh: Wait. So forgetting helps?

Ms. Okafor: A little forgetting, yes.

Josh: Okay, but I don't have time to study every day. I have my job, choir practice...

Mei: Josh. You found four hours last night.

Josh: ...Okay. Fair. But not Thursdays. Thursday is choir, and I'm not giving that up.

Ms. Okafor: It's not about more time. It's using time differently. For example, four short sessions instead of one long night.

Josh: Mm. And staying up until two? Is that bad too?

Ms. Okafor: It is. Sleep is part of the learning process. While you sleep, your brain makes the day's memories stronger. So if you cut your sleep, you lose some of the benefit of studying.

Josh: So my brain didn't even save it?

Ms. Okafor: Some of it. In other words, the problem was never your hours. It's how you use them. In my first year, I reread a whole chemistry book in one night. I got six out of twenty.

Josh: Wait, you? Six out of twenty? ...Okay. That actually makes me feel a little better.

Mei: See? You're not hopeless.

Josh: Thanks... I think.

Ms. Okafor: So, Josh. What's your plan for the next quiz?

Josh: Um, it's in two weeks. So, first, after every class, I'll close my notes and write what I remember. Then twice a week... no, three times. Monday, Wednesday, and Saturday. I'll quiz myself on the old chapters. And the night before the quiz, I'll actually sleep.

Ms. Okafor: Three times a week. That's a great plan. Just one more thing. It'll feel harder than rereading. When it gets hard, don't stop. Persistence is important here.

Josh: Got it. Mei, can we study together on Monday? You can quiz me.

Mei: Sure. But only if you quiz me too. My history quiz is on Wednesday, and I haven't started.

Josh: You? Ha. Fine. I'm actually motivated now. Which is weird, after a five out of fifteen.

Ms. Okafor: It's not weird at all. Good luck, you two.
