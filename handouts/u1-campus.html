Culture Night: Keep It or Change It?

Two members of the International Culture Club meet the student activities advisor to plan their Culture Night performance.

Name: ______________________   Date: ____________   Score: ____ / 12

1. Before you listen: key words

traditional adjectivefollowing customs that have existed for a long time
generation nounall the people born at about the same time
adapt (to) verbto change something so it fits a new situation
heritage nountraditions, art and history passed down from the past
challenge noun / verbsomething difficult that tests you; to question whether something is right
ensure verbto make certain that something happens
context nounthe situation in which something happens
perspective nouna way of thinking about something
barrier nounsomething that stops people from doing or getting something
cooperate verbto work together to reach a goal

2. While you listen: notes

Listen for signposts (first, but, the main reason is, so to sum up). Write key words, not sentences.

Problem / situation
Opinions (who thinks what?)
Reasons & examples
Decision / next step

3. Questions

1. What is the conversation mainly about?

  1. Adding a modern part and giving the traditional dance new music
  2. Adding a modern part and keeping the traditional dance as it is
  3. Adding a modern part and cutting the traditional dance in half
  4. Adding a modern part and teaching everyone simpler fan steps

2. What does Mei say about the number of students?

  1. Six, because her roommate signed up this morning.
  2. Seven, because her roommate signed up this morning.
  3. Six, because her classmate signed up last night.
  4. Seven, because her classmate signed up last night.

3. What is Sofia's main point about the old music?

  1. They like it, but can't follow the words, so don't know the story.
  2. They like it, but can't count the steps, so don't know when to turn.
  3. They like it, but can't sing along, so don't feel part of it.
  4. They like it, but can't hear the beat, so don't know when to move.

4. When Sofia says, "I'm not saying we change all of it!", how does Mei react?

  1. She says, "Okay… maybe," and starts to accept the idea.
  2. She asks Mr. Tanaka to decide which idea is better.
  3. She says sorry and explains that she is just worried.
  4. She laughs and says that Sofia always says that.

5. What reason does Mei give right after "Because it's my heritage"?

  1. Her grandmother asked her never to change any of the steps.
  2. People in her grandmother's village still dance it the same way.
  3. She wants to teach it to her own children one day.
  4. She's the only one in her family who still knows the steps.

6. How does Mr. Tanaka respond to Sofia's example about last year?

  1. He blames himself: he told the group to explain in their language.
  2. He blames himself: he told the group to skip their introduction.
  3. He blames himself: he left the song's meaning out of the program.
  4. He blames himself: he forgot to give the group a microphone.

7. What does Mei say about the way her grandmother taught her the dance?

  1. She played a video and taught the last part sitting down.
  2. She played a recording and taught the last part more slowly.
  3. She played a video and taught the last part more slowly.
  4. She played a recording and taught the last part sitting down.

8. What does Mei mean when she says, "Her steps, her way"?

  1. She wants the dance as her grandmother taught it, changes and all.
  2. She wants the dance as her grandmother first learned it, with live music.
  3. She wants her grandmother to watch the show and decide what to change.
  4. She wants her grandmother to choose who dances each part.

9. How does Sofia react to Mei's idea about the order?

  1. She agrees, because the audience will still be fresh for the modern part.
  2. She agrees, because the new dancers won't wait backstage and get nervous.
  3. She agrees, because Mei will have more time to get ready backstage.
  4. She agrees, because the modern music will bring people into the hall.

10. What will Sofia probably do when Mei explains the dance?

  1. Tell the story again in English
  2. Show photos of Mei's grandmother's village
  3. Hold up the fans the dancers will use
  4. Lead the new dancers in the modern part

11. What is the club's final plan, in order?

  1. Mei explains the dance, then the traditional dance, then a modern part
  2. The traditional dance, then Mei explains it, then a modern part
  3. A modern part, then Mei explains the dance, then the traditional dance
  4. Mei explains the dance, then a modern part, then the traditional dance

12. What will Mei probably do on Sunday?

  1. Ask her grandmother about the story of the dance
  2. Ask her grandmother for the old drum music
  3. Ask her grandmother to help choose the modern music
  4. Practice the fan dance with her grandmother by phone

4. After you listen: discuss

  1. Is there a dance, song, or custom in your family or culture that you want to keep exactly the same? Why?
  2. Do you agree with Mei or with Sofia? Is it okay to add something new to a traditional performance?
  3. Think of a tradition in your culture that each generation has changed a little. What changed, and what stayed the same?

Transcript

Mr. Tanaka: Come in, come in. Mei, Sofia, have a seat. So, you're here about the International Culture Club's show for Culture Night?

Sofia: Yes. Thanks for meeting with us, Mr. Tanaka. We, um… kind of have a problem.

Mei: It's not really a problem. It's more like… we don't agree.

Mr. Tanaka: Okay. That happens a lot when people really care about something. Tell me about it.

Mei: Well, I want our club to do the fan dance my grandmother taught me. It's a traditional dance from her village. I learned it when I was eight.

Mr. Tanaka: Oh, nice.

Sofia: And I love that dance. Really, I do. But the main problem is people. Only five students have signed up, and the dance needs eight dancers.

Mei: Six, actually. My roommate signed up this morning.

Mr. Tanaka: Oh, good. Six. And you need eight?

Sofia: Yeah, eight. So we're still two people short.

Mr. Tanaka: Hmm. That's a real challenge. And Culture Night is in three weeks.

Sofia: Right. So my idea is to add some modern music. The reason is, for a lot of new students, the old music is kind of a barrier. It's not that they don't like it. They do! They just can't hear the beat, so they don't know when to move.

Mei: But if we change the music, it's not my grandmother's dance anymore.

Sofia: I'm not saying we change all of it!

Mei: I know, I know. Sorry. I'm just… worried.

Mr. Tanaka: That's okay. Mei, can I ask you something? Why is it so important to keep it exactly the same?

Mei: Because it's my heritage. My grandmother is getting older, and I'm the only one in my family who still knows the steps. If I change them, they could be lost.

Mr. Tanaka: I see. In other words, you want to ensure the steps are passed on correctly.

Mei: Yes. Exactly.

Mr. Tanaka: And Sofia, what about you? What's your perspective?

Sofia: Well, I'm thinking about the audience. For example, last year one group sang a really long song in their language. It was beautiful, but nobody explained it. And people just… looked at their phones.

Mr. Tanaka: Ah. To be honest, that was my fault. I told that group to skip their introduction, to save time.

Mei: Oh. I didn't know that.

Sofia: So what I'm saying is, if people can't connect with a performance, it doesn't really reach them. Something they know, like modern music, can help them connect.

Mr. Tanaka: Okay. Mei, one more question. Did your grandmother learn the dance in exactly the same way from her mother?

Mei: Um… actually, no. Now that I think about it… her mother danced to live drums. But my grandmother taught me with a recording on her phone.

Sofia: Oh, really? So the music already changed.

Mei: Yeah. And… one step changed, too. My grandmother's knees hurt, so she does the last part sitting down. That's how she taught me.

Mr. Tanaka: Interesting. So every generation adapts something. Even the steps.

Mei: Hmm. Okay… maybe. I guess what matters to me is my grandmother's version. Her steps, her way.

Sofia: Then what if we keep her version exactly the same? First, we do the whole traditional dance, with the traditional music. Then, at the end, we add a short modern part. Just two minutes.

Mei: At the end? Hmm. Can we switch it? Do the modern part first. People remember the ending, right?

Sofia: Oh, that's even better. Then the new dancers don't have to wait backstage and get nervous.

Mr. Tanaka: And it might help with your dancer problem, too. Anyone can join a two-minute part. How long is the traditional dance?

Mei: About six minutes.

Mr. Tanaka: And each club gets ten minutes on stage. So six minutes, plus two… that's eight. You still have two minutes left.

Sofia: Ooh. What can we do with two more minutes?

Mr. Tanaka: Well, here's one idea. People enjoy a show more when they have some context. You know, where the dance comes from, and what it means. What if Mei explains that before her dance?

Mei: Me? On stage? Talking? Okay… but I'll tell it in my grandmother's language. That's how she tells it to me. And, um, Sofia, your English is better than mine…

Sofia: Ha! Okay, okay. I've got you.

Mr. Tanaka: Sounds good. So to sum up: first, a short modern part that everyone can join. Second, Mei explains the dance and where it comes from. And finally, the traditional dance, exactly as her grandmother taught it.

Sofia: Perfect.

Mei: Okay. I think that works. Actually… I kind of like it.

Mr. Tanaka: Good. Now, who's choosing the modern music?

Sofia: Me! But Mei gets the final word.

Mei: Deal. Oh, and my grandmother calls me every Sunday. You should hear the pop songs she dances to in her kitchen.

Sofia: Wait, really? Then she's on the music team!

Mei: Ha! I'll tell her.

Mr. Tanaka: You two will be fine. Just keep cooperating like this, and it'll be a great show.
