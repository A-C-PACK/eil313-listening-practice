EIL313 Listening Practice · Unit 3 · Campus conversation

Rent Is Going Up

Sofia and her friend Josh visit Mr. Tanaka in the off-campus housing office because Sofia's rent is going up.

Name: ______________________   Date: ____________   Score: ____ / 12

1. Before you listen: key words

impact nouna strong effect
policy nounan official plan or set of rules made by a government or organization
density nounhow many people or things are in an area
infrastructure nounthe basic systems a city needs: roads, pipes, drains, power
rely (on) verbto need or depend on someone or something
alternative nounanother possible choice
temporary adjectivelasting for only a short time
resource nounsomething valuable that people can use
controversial adjectivecausing a lot of disagreement
adjacent adjectivenext to or near

2. While you listen: notes

Listen for signposts (first, but, the main reason is, so to sum up). Write key words, not sentences.

Problem / situation
Opinions (who thinks what?)
Reasons & examples
Decision / next step

3. Questions

1. What is the conversation mainly about?

  1. Why rent near campus is rising, and who is to blame for it
  2. Sofia's housing choices, and the problem that comes with each one
  3. Sofia's housing choices, and why sharing is the best one
  4. The tower plan, and why students and neighbors disagree about it

2. What are Mr. Tanaka's first two reasons for higher rent?

  1. More students, and the one new building rents to tourists
  2. More students, and building costs that went up a lot
  3. Old roads and pipes, and too many people in one area
  4. More students, and old apartments being torn down

3. Where does the company now want to build the tower?

  1. On the empty land by the main gate
  2. On cheaper land farther away from campus
  3. On land right next to the library
  4. On the old field behind the gym

4. Mr. Tanaka calls the tower plan controversial. Why does Josh then say, "I play soccer there every Saturday"?

  1. To agree with the students who want to keep their field
  2. To suggest that the first site by the main gate is better
  3. To show that the tower would cost him something he enjoys
  4. To agree with the neighbors that the area is too crowded

5. How does Sofia react to Josh's idea of sharing a bigger apartment?

  1. She refuses, because she grew up sharing with four sisters
  2. She likes it, but says her last roommate left without paying
  3. She likes it, but says the houses near campus are all taken
  4. She refuses, because big houses near campus cost too much

6. How does Mr. Tanaka respond to Josh's bus idea?

  1. He says no, because she would save very little money
  2. He says it can work, but warns her about the bus times
  3. He says yes, because rent out there is much lower
  4. He lets Sofia decide, and gives her a bus map

7. How often does the bus come?

  1. Every forty minutes
  2. Every fifteen minutes
  3. Every sixty minutes
  4. Every ninety minutes

8. Why does Sofia mention her library job?

  1. To show that the bus times fit her work hours
  2. To show that she could study on the long ride
  3. To show that the last bus leaves before her work ends
  4. To show that she could switch to an earlier shift

9. What does Mr. Tanaka say about the campus rooms for students like Sofia?

  1. Pets aren't allowed in them
  2. They're only for first-year students
  3. Students can stay there for one year
  4. Students must share them with three others

10. How does Sofia respond to Josh's last suggestion?

  1. She treats it as a joke, then gives her own plan
  2. She teases him, then changes his plan a little
  3. She takes his joke seriously and makes it her plan
  4. She likes it, but asks Mr. Tanaka to check it first

11. What plan does Sofia describe at the end?

  1. Post a roommate note today, and keep a dorm room as a backup
  2. Apply for a dorm room today, and keep the roommate board as a backup
  3. Post a roommate note today, and speak at the tower meeting tonight
  4. Apply for a dorm room today, and speak at the tower meeting tonight

12. What does Sofia's answer, "Now, here," suggest about her?

  1. She has no free time at home because of her library job
  2. She still feels unsure and wants Mr. Tanaka's advice as she writes
  3. She wants her evening free to take her cat to her aunt's
  4. She no longer feels stuck and wants to start right away

4. After you listen: discuss

  1. Where do students live in your home country: with family, in dorms, or in shared apartments? Why?
  2. Would you share an apartment with people you have never met? What would help you feel comfortable?
  3. Should a town build a tall apartment building next to a university? What are the good and bad sides?

Transcript

Mr. Tanaka: Hi there, come on in. Welcome to the off-campus housing office.

Sofia: Hi, Mr. Tanaka. I'm Sofia, and this is my friend Josh. Um, I've got a housing problem.

Josh: I'm just here for support. And ideas. I've got lots of ideas.

Sofia: He really does.

Mr. Tanaka: Ha. Ideas are welcome. So, Sofia, what's going on?

Sofia: My landlord just told me the rent's going up next semester. Like, a lot. I can't pay that, so I have to move out.

Mr. Tanaka: Oh, I'm sorry. You're not alone, though. I've seen about twenty students this week with the same problem.

Sofia: Twenty? Really?

Mr. Tanaka: Well, let me check my list... no, twenty-six. Twenty-six, just this week.

Josh: But why is everything so expensive now? Rent wasn't this bad when I started.

Mr. Tanaka: Good question. First, our campus is growing. More students come every year, and they all need somewhere to live.

Sofia: Mm-hm. My classes are definitely bigger this year.

Mr. Tanaka: The second reason is that almost nothing new is being built. In five years, the town has built only one new apartment building.

Josh: Wait, only one? In five years?

Mr. Tanaka: Just one. And here's the strange part. It rents rooms to tourists, not students. So, more students, but no new homes for them. Because of that, landlords can ask for more money.

Sofia: Tourists? Wow. And what about that big tower?

Mr. Tanaka: Ah, the tower. That's the third part of the story. A company wants to build a twelve-story tower adjacent to campus, I mean right next to it. At first, they wanted the land by the main gate. But now the plan is the old field behind the gym.

Josh: Oh, that's perfect! Hundreds of new apartments. Problem solved, right?

Mr. Tanaka: Well... not so fast. The plan is very controversial. Some people love it, and some people really don't.

Sofia: Why would anyone be against more apartments?

Mr. Tanaka: The main worry is density. In other words, a lot of people in one small area. Neighbors worry about traffic and noise. And they say the infrastructure, I mean the roads and water pipes, is too old. And here's a surprise. Many students are against it too. They play soccer on that field.

Josh: Wait, the field behind the gym? I play soccer there every Saturday. Hmm... Build it anyway. People need homes more than I need a field.

Mr. Tanaka: Ha. Not everyone agrees. The town has argued for two years, and nothing's been built. There's a town meeting about it tonight. Meanwhile, the impact on students is real.

Sofia: So basically... I'm stuck.

Mr. Tanaka: No, no, you're not stuck. You have some alternatives. Other choices. Let's look at them one at a time.

Josh: Okay, I've got one. Share a bigger apartment! Find three roommates and split the rent. Easy.

Sofia: Honestly? No thanks. I grew up with four sisters and one bathroom. I want my own door for once.

Mr. Tanaka: Fair enough. But sharing saves money. For example, a four-bedroom house costs less per person than a studio. Our roommate board is a useful resource, if you change your mind.

Sofia: Maybe. But not this time.

Josh: Okay, second idea. Live farther away, where rent is cheaper, and just take the bus.

Mr. Tanaka: Honestly? I wouldn't. Rent out there is about a hundred dollars less a month. But if you rely on the bus, a bus pass is ninety dollars a month.

Josh: Ninety? Wow. And how often does it come? Like once an hour?

Mr. Tanaka: No, every fifteen minutes, actually. That part's great. But the last one leaves at nine at night.

Sofia: Nine? My library job ends at ten. And I'd only save ten dollars a month. No way.

Josh: Oh. Hmm. Yeah, okay.

Mr. Tanaka: Okay, the last option. Temporary campus housing. We keep a few dorm rooms for students like you.

Sofia: Really? I thought the dorms were full.

Mr. Tanaka: Most of them are. But here's the thing. Our policy is one semester only. It's temporary. Oh, and no pets in the dorms. Sorry.

Sofia: No pets? Okay, my cat can stay with my aunt.

Mr. Tanaka: Hmm. And the rooms go fast. Apply by this Friday.

Josh: Or go to that town meeting tonight and tell them to build the tower. Ha. Kidding.

Sofia: Wait. That's not a bad idea. We're the ones paying the rent.

Josh: Hey, I was joking!

Sofia: Too late. So let me make sure I've got it. First, I apply for a dorm room today. Then, tonight, I go to the meeting and speak for the tower.

Mr. Tanaka: That's it. We need more students at those meetings.

Sofia: Okay. That feels possible. Do you have the dorm application?

Mr. Tanaka: Right here. It takes about ten minutes. Want to do it now, or at home?

Sofia: Now, here. And Josh, you're coming with me tonight.

Josh: Fine. But I'm wearing my soccer shirt.

Mr. Tanaka: Ha. The meeting's at city hall, at seven. Pens are right there.

Sofia: Thanks, both of you. Really.
