Reread or Test Yourself? Reading a Memory Chart

Three classmates in a study group talk about a chart from their psychology class experiment before writing a paragraph about it.

  • Audio: 6 min
  • 136 words/min
  • 2 listens
  • 12 questions
Step 1 of 6

Get ready

Meet the speakers, learn the key words, and read the test rules.

Who is speaking?

  • JJoshStudent. He read the handout about the class experiment.
  • MMeiStudent. She asks questions about the chart.
  • SSofiaStudent. She has her own ideas about how to study.

Listen for research cues like "participants," "compared to," and "significantly better": they tell you who was studied, what was compared, and how strong the result is.Strategy S7: Recognize research and evidence cues

Study the graph

Take a minute to read the title, the axes and the lines. You will see the graph again during the test.

Test rules

  • You can read all 12 questions before and while you listen.
  • You can play the recording two times. You can't pause, skip or slow it down, just like in a real test.
  • There is no transcript during the test. After you finish, you will see your score, go through each mistake, and listen again with the transcript.
  • Look at the graph while you listen. Some questions need the graph and the audio.
